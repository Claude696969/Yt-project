# Roblox "In This Town" videos: most viewed on YouTube

View counts were read from YouTube's public search results on **2026-11-25**. View counts change by the hour, so treat them as a snapshot. Raw data for all 218 Shorts and 44 long-form videos is in [`data/in_this_town_videos.json`](data/in_this_town_videos.json).

## Key takeaways

- **The format is new and still growing.** Almost every top video is under about three months old. The long-form compilations that lead the rankings are one day to three weeks old.
- **The biggest videos are Shorts.** Nine Shorts have more than 4M views each. The top long-form compilation has 2.5M.
- **"In This World…" is the same format.** Two of the three most-viewed Shorts are titled "In This World", and compilations usually put both phrases in the title.
- **The "HUSS HUSS HUSS" offshoot** comes from the "in this town everybody goes out partying except me" meme, which started on TikTok around mid-November 2026. It produced many Shorts with 0.1M–2.3M views, but none of the 9M-class hits.
- **A second wave is forming.** Compilations, reaction videos ("Roasting Cringe 'In This Town' Stories") and tutorials ("How to make viral In This Town Shorts with AI/Claude") are already appearing. The format is moving from fresh into saturated.

## Why these videos work

These patterns come from comparing the titles and numbers above. The videos themselves weren't watched, so read these as informed inference.

1. **The title is the hook.** The usual shape is "In This Town/World, [one strange rule]…", for example: you can't speak; walking alone is deadly; secrets turn into mice; everyone has 3 steal buttons; mirrors show your happiest moment.
2. **High-stakes or mystery rules do best.** The 4M+ Shorts are about death, danger, sacrifice, or a visible stat like IQ, happiness or steal buttons. Comedic or "huss" premises stay below about 2.3M.
3. **A twist ending.** The narrator is usually the one exception ("…except me"), which gives each Short a payoff.
4. **Short titles with few hashtags.** The biggest titles carry only `#roblox #shorts` (sometimes `#viral`, `#rblx #stories`). Hashtag-stuffed titles mostly sit under 200K.
5. **Compilations multiply reach.** Creators stitch 20–40 stories into a 15–90 minute upload with numbered parts (#1, #2…), and several reach 0.5–2.5M within one to three weeks.
6. **Cheap to make.** The videos use Roblox avatars, simple animation and AI or TTS narration. Tutorials on automating them with AI already exist, which suggests a coming flood of similar videos.
7. **Meme crossover helps.** The TikTok "huss" sound shows the format can absorb trending audio. Hooking onto a live meme early gave 1M+ results.

## How this was collected

- Four Sonnet sub-agents searched the web for the exact phrase, top channels, title variants and success patterns. Their web-fetch tool couldn't reach YouTube, so they contributed leads and trend context but no verified numbers.
- The numbers were collected with [`scripts/yt_search.py`](scripts/yt_search.py) (long-form) and [`scripts/yt_shorts.py`](scripts/yt_shorts.py) (Shorts). Both scripts parse public YouTube search pages sorted by view count and by relevance.
- YouTube search shows only the top few dozen results per query, so this is the most-viewed set **that search surfaces**, not a complete census. Some huge Shorts that have no "in this town" text in their titles may be missing.
- Unverified claims from search snippets aren't counted above: the original TikTok "huss" clip reportedly had 36–41M views in under a week, and one creator claims 140M views from 30 Shorts.
